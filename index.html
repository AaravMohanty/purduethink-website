Who we are

Student-run consulting at Purdue since 2016.

PurdueTHINK is Purdue’s leading and oldest student-run consultancy. For over a decade, we’ve partnered with organizations across campus to solve their hardest problems, giving our members real, hands-on project experience while helping build a better Purdue.

We also invest heavily in our members beyond client work. Internally, that means casework and mentorship from some of the most accomplished consultants on campus. Externally, it means an industry network that opens doors most students don’t see until years into their career. And underneath all of it is a community that actually shows up for each other, in casework, on projects, and everywhere in between.

Consulting 101

What is consulting?

What consultants actually do, what the work asks of you, and how case interviews tend to run.

The Field

In the business of renting out brains.

According to the Oxford Dictionary, consulting is giving expert advice to people working in a specific field. But consultants rarely use set definitions.

How an engagement works

A structured semester, end to end.

Every project follows the same arc, roughly eight weeks from kickoff to final delivery.

Week 1 01
Scoping
Define the problem and success metrics with the client.
Weeks 2–3 02
Discovery
Research, stakeholder interviews, and data gathering.
Weeks 4–5 03
Analysis
Frameworks, modeling, and pulling it into findings.
Weeks 6–7 04
Strategy
Build the recommendation set and implementation roadmap.
Week 8 05
Delivery
Final presentation, deck, and handoff to the client.
What it takes

What traits does a consultant have?

Consulting is closer to studying at a university, where you are continuously learning something new. It takes strong functional skills to solve problems across many industries, and most of the work happens inside a team.

01
Strong communication
Clients span very different corporate cultures, so reading the room matters as much as the analysis does.
02
Leadership
Holding the scope, keeping the team moving, and presenting the recommendation at the end.
03
Drive and initiative
Eight weeks is a long time to hold momentum, and teams lean on the people who bring it.
04
A team player
Almost all of the work happens in a team, so how you work with people matters as much as the output.
Interview process

The interview process.

What we ask in a THINK interview, what makes a consulting interview different, and how to prepare for the case.

The Interview

What makes consulting interviews different?

Interviews for consulting firms usually consist of a behavioral interview and a case interview. A case interview is when you are given a business problem and asked to solve it. Although it may sound simple, case interviews often intimidate people, and they can be intense and sometimes unpredictable. A few things that tend to help are below.

The interview

Forty minutes, in two parts.

This is mostly us getting to know you. You might be asked about a time you improved a team, or talked people into a decision they were not sold on at first. We are also curious what brought you here.

There may be brain teasers and case-style questions. If you have questions about THINK, this is a good time to ask them.

Part one 20 MIN
Behavioral

Much like a regular job interview. Most people find it helps to think through a few examples beforehand and say them out loud once or twice.

Indeed · The STAR method → Hacking the Case Interview · Fit prep →
Part two 20 MIN
Case scenario

A business problem, worked through out loud. There is rarely one right answer, and we are more interested in how you structure it than where you land. Questions and notes are both fine.

Ace the Case

How to ace case interviews.

01
Always ask questions
The interviewer will give you the most important information about the case, but they will also give you the chance to get more by asking.
02
Structure your answer
There is usually more than one approach to a case, but you need to show your way of thinking. Gather your thoughts, jot down notes, and outline the logical structure of your solution.
03
Learn your case types
There is an almost infinite number of cases you could be given, but there are recognizable case “types” that become clear once you practice.
04
Practice your cases
Reading through cases, practicing with a friend, doing case competitions. These are just some of the ways to get comfortable with case interviews.
05
Join a consulting club on campus.
